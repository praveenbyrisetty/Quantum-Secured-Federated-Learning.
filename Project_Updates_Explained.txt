====================================================================
PROJECT UPDATES: HOW WE FIXED THE AI'S LEARNING CURVE
(A Simple Guide for the Team)
====================================================================

THE PROBLEM WE FACED:
When we first ran the project, the AI curve was a perfectly flat line at 88% accuracy. The Loss (Error) wasn't dropping. It looked like the AI wasn't learning anything—and that's because it wasn't! It was stuck in a "lazy" state, and our security server was accidentally deleting its progress. 

Here are the 3 major changes we made to fix it, explained simply:

--------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGE 1: Stopping the AI from "Cheating" (Class Weights)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Issue: Our dataset (HAM10000) is heavily imbalanced. About 67% of all the images are just one class: "Normal Moles" (nv). The AI quickly realized that if it just blindly guessed "Normal Mole" on every single patient, it would instantly get an 88% accuracy score without having to learn what actual cancer looks like!
* The Fix: We added "Class Weights" to the AI's math (the Loss Function). This acts as a severe penalty if the AI lazily guesses "Normal Mole" when the patient actually has a rare cancer. This forced the AI to actually study the visual differences between all 7 skin diseases.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGE 2: Fixing the "Paranoid" Security Server (IID Data Splitting)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Issue: Our security algorithms (Trimmed Mean and Krum) protect our system by comparing all the hospitals. If one hospital sends data that looks drastically different, the server assumes it's a hacker and deletes the data. In our old code, Hospital A was the ONLY hospital that had Melanoma images. When Hospital A tried to teach the server about Melanoma, the server looked at B and C (who had no Melanoma images), assumed Hospital A was a hacker, and wiped its memory!
* The Fix: We changed the data splitting to "IID". Now, the massive pile of patient images is randomly shuffled and dealt out evenly to all 3 hospitals. Because they all see a mix of diseases, their AI brains agree with each other. The security server (Krum) sees the agreement, knows there are no hackers, and successfully merges their knowledge.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGE 3: Giving the AI's Brain Room to Grow (Norm Clipping)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Issue: We kept getting a "Norm Anomaly" warning. The "Norm" is a measurement of how big the AI's mathematical brain has grown. Our server had a strict security rule: "If the brain size goes over 1500, assume it's a virus and shrink it!"
* The Fix: We increased the `NORM_THRESHOLD` limit from 1500 to 5000. Our custom AI is massive (over 8.3 million parameters). As it rapidly started learning, its brain naturally grew past the tiny 1500 limit. By raising the ceiling to 5000, we gave our 8.3 million numbers the physical room they needed to capture complex cancer features.

====================================================================
PHASE 2 UPDATES: STABILITY & VISUAL ANALYTICS
====================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGE 4: Enhancing Model Stability (AdamW & Gradient Protection)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Issue: Integrating strong Differential Privacy noise alongside a dataset with severe class imbalance (where some diseases are much rarer than others) inherently causes "numerical instability." The mathematical learning gradients were occasionally becoming too aggressive and destabilizing the model's global convergence.
* The Fix: We implemented a three-layer "stability net" to force the AI to learn smoothly:
  1. Optimizer Upgrade: We upgraded the core learning engine from basic SGD to the much more advanced `AdamW` optimizer (Learning Rate = 0.001), which acts as a mathematical shock-absorber against the privacy noise.
  2. Weight Smoothing: We refined the mathematical class weights in our Loss Function to gently guide the AI towards minority classes without causing violent gradient explosions.
  3. Gradient Validation Firewall: We hardcoded an emergency validation checkpoint inside the local training loop. If any batch of images produces corrupted math due to extreme noise scaling, the system safely discards that specific batch (`optimizer.zero_grad()`) perfectly preserving the integrity of the master model.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGE 5: Professional UI Visualizations
--------------------------------------------------------------------
* The Issue: We had massive numbers representing our security layers, but they were hard to prove during a presentation. 
* The Fix: We programmed `server.py` to dynamically generate two powerful UI graphs at the end of training:
  1. CHSH Eavesdropping Graph: A line chart proving the Quantum Entanglement score stays safely above the 2.0 threshold over all rounds.
  2. Privacy vs. Accuracy Trade-off: A striking bar chart that specifically calculates and visually proves exactly how much accuracy we traded to keep the patients completely secure via Differential Privacy compared to an unsecure baseline system.

====================================================================
HOW TO INCREASE ACCURACY FURTHER IN THE FUTURE
====================================================================
If we decide to expand this project or are asked how we could push the accuracy from 50% up to 80%+, here is exactly what we would do:

1. Use Transfer Learning (Pre-Trained Models)
Right now, we built a custom Convolutional Neural Network from scratch. It is acting like a newborn baby trying to learn what a mole 
looks like. To massively increase accuracy, we would swap out our custom model for a giant, famous Google/Facebook model 
(like ResNet-50 or DenseNet-121) that has already spent years looking at millions of photos online. 

2. Loosen the Privacy Restriction (Increase Epsilon)
Our project prioritizes Patient Privacy over perfect accuracy. We are injecting Differential Privacy noise 
(Epsilon = 5.0) into the AI's brain. If we wanted higher accuracy, we could increase the Epsilon budget to 50.0 or 100.0. 
This would inject less "chaotic noise" into the brain, making it smarter, but slightly less secure against reverse-engineering attacks.

3. Train for More Rounds 
Currently, we only run the simulation for 3 to 10 rounds to save time. If we let the AI run overnight for 100 to 200 rounds, the learning 
curve would have time to fully mature and stabilize at a much higher percentage.

4. Provide Higher Resolution Images
To make the AI train quickly on a standard computer, we squashed the massive, highly detailed medical 
images down to small 128x128 pixel squares. Real dermatologists need extremely high resolution to spot tiny 
skin cancer details. If we had powerful supercomputers, we could feed the AI 512x512 images, giving it the
 literal pixels it needs to spot the cancer.
